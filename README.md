# UY Africa

## Knowledge. Capability. Action.

**UY.Africa** *(pronounced "you why dot Africa")* is an open African organisation dedicated to expanding access to computing, research, technology, education and technological opportunity.

We believe Africa should not have to own the world's largest computers to participate in the world's most important technologies.

We believe that a young person with a modest computer, an Internet connection, curiosity and access to knowledge can begin learning the same fundamental technologies that power the world's most advanced computing systems.

We believe African technological development is strongest when it is collaborative, open, experimental and inclusive.

And we believe that Africa already contains an extraordinary community of people doing this work.

**UY exists to help connect them.**

---

## What does UY mean?

UY brings together two ideas found across African languages and cultures:

**Ulwazi** — Skill / knowledge.

**Uwezo** — Capability, ability and capacity.

**Ubuntu** — I am, because we are.

Together with:

**Yenza** — Do, make and create.

The philosophy is simple:

> **Knowledge → Capability → Action**

Learn.

Build.

Research.

Create.

Share.

Then help someone else do the same.

# One Million Research Activations

UY's long-term, moonshot ambition is to enable:

## One Million Research Activations

across Africa.

A research activation may begin with something as simple as a Raspberry Pi, a microcontroller, an old computer, an open-source project, a student competition or a community laboratory.

It may eventually lead to a research project, a scientific publication, a startup, an open-source contribution, a new technology, a PhD, a job or an entirely new field of African innovation.

The technology is not the point.

**The activation is.**

---

# The first activation

## One Million Raspberry Pis

Our founding campaign is an ambitious long-term effort to expand access to affordable computing through Raspberry Pi and other appropriate low-cost computing platforms.

Raspberry Pi is our starting point, not our boundary.

UY is technology agnostic.

We welcome:

* Raspberry Pi
* ARM
* x86
* RISC-V
* microcontrollers
* GPUs
* servers
* HPC
* edge computing
* cloud computing
* distributed computing
* quantum computing
* open hardware
* technologies that do not yet exist.

If it helps people learn, build, research or create, we want to explore it.

# What UY does

UY does not seek to replace existing African initiatives.

We seek to:

* connect them;
* amplify them;
* support them;
* document them;
* co-promote them;
* help them access resources;
* facilitate collaboration;
* create opportunities for students and researchers;
* support open-source development;
* help successful projects scale;
* connect research with entrepreneurship;
* and help African technological talent remain connected to one another.

The ecosystem belongs to everyone.

UY is an enabling layer within it.

# Our principles

## Ubuntu

We succeed together.

Technology should strengthen communities rather than isolate them.

## Ulwazi

Knowledge should circulate.

Whenever possible, knowledge, educational resources, software, research and lessons should be shared openly.

## Uwezo

Capability matters.

Our goal is not merely to provide technology, but to build the ability to understand, operate, modify, research and create technology.

## Yenza

Do.

Experiment.

Build.

Fail.

Learn.

Try again.

## Openness

We prefer open standards, open-source software, open hardware and open educational resources where practical.

## Inclusion

Computing opportunity should not depend upon wealth, geography, gender, language, institution or access to elite infrastructure.

## Collaboration

No single organisation can build Africa's technological future.

We will work with universities, schools, governments, NGOs, communities, companies, researchers, technology organisations and individual builders.

## Technology agnosticism

There is no sacred technology stack.

Use what works.

Measure it.

Learn from it.

Share what you learn.

# From student to researcher to founder

UY is designed around a long-term pipeline.

A student might begin by participating in a computing competition.

They may then build infrastructure.

They may conduct research.

They may publish.

They may start a company.

They may join a university.

They may become an engineer.

They may become a researcher.

They may return as a mentor.

The journey does not end when a programme ends.

It becomes part of the ecosystem.

# Open by default

UY intends to make its own work a learning resource.

Where privacy, security, contractual obligations and law permit, we intend to publish:

* source code;
* infrastructure;
* technical architectures;
* educational resources;
* research;
* impact methodology;
* aggregate data;
* budgets;
* governance policies;
* deployment guides;
* lessons learned;
* failures and abandoned experiments.

We want young Africans to be able to study not only what UY builds, but **how UY itself was built**.

# UY as an experiment

UY is itself an experiment.

Can an African organisation be:

* globally connected;
* locally grounded;
* technologically ambitious;
* financially transparent;
* open-source;
* research-driven;
* institutionally sustainable;
* and built with modest resources?

We intend to find out.
