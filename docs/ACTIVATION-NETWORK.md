# UY Activation Network

## From One Activation to One Million

**Status:** Founding concept
**Date:** 2026-08-31

---

# The Core Idea

UY does not intend to scale by building a large central organisation.

UY scales by enabling people to **create activations themselves**.

A student participates in an UY activation.

They learn.

They build.

They compete.

They discover something they care about.

Then they become a volunteer, mentor, organiser or founder of another activation.

That activation creates more participants.

Those participants create more activations.

The network compounds.

> **One activation creates participants.**
> **Participants create activations.**
> **Activations create capability.**
> **Capability creates research, companies and opportunities.**
> **Those people return to create the next generation.**

This is the mechanism through which UY can pursue its long-term ambition of:

# One Million Research Activations

---

# For Us, By Us, With Us

UY's primary operating principle is:

> **For us. By us. With us.**

UY is built around the belief that African young people should not merely be recipients of technology programmes.

They should be:

* participants;
* builders;
* organisers;
* teachers;
* mentors;
* researchers;
* entrepreneurs;
* community leaders;
* and eventually funders and institutional partners.

The objective is not to build a permanent central organisation that does everything.

The objective is to build a community capable of **doing things itself**.

This is Ubuntu expressed as an operating model.

> **We build together.**
> **We learn together.**
> **We compete together.**
> **We teach one another.**
> **We create opportunities for those who come after us.**

# Open Programme Materials

UY's programmes should be designed to be reusable.

Where possible, materials will be:

* open source;
* openly licensed;
* free;
* reproducible;
* hardware-flexible;
* documented;
* translated where practical;
* designed for offline use where practical.

A student in Cape Town should be able to use the same material as a student in Nairobi.

A student in Lagos should be able to adapt it for their environment.

A student in Kampala should be able to improve it.

A student in Accra should be able to fork it.

A student in São Paulo or Mumbai should eventually be able to use it too.

The material belongs to the ecosystem.

# Example: One Million Raspberry Pis

The first flagship activation can demonstrate this model.

A Raspberry Pi challenge could begin with:

**1,000 students**

participating remotely.

Participants work through openly available preparation material and complete an initial challenge.

Because physical hardware may be limited, qualification can involve:

* remote challenges;
* software simulations;
* online competitions;
* technical submissions;
* project proposals;
* demonstrations;
* community contribution.

The strongest participants advance.

For example:

```text
1,000 participants
       ↓
200 qualifying teams
       ↓
40 regional teams
       ↓
20 national finalists
       ↓
continental finalists
       ↓
African champion teams
       ↓
international competition
```

The precise numbers will depend on funding, geography, logistics and the competition.

The principle remains:

> **Make participation broad and accessible, while making advancement meritocratic.**

# Regional and National Events

UY should deliberately rotate physical events between partner institutions.

A continental event might be hosted by:

* AIMS;
* a university;
* a university of technology;
* CHPC;
* another research institution;
* a technology company;
* a community organisation;
* or another suitable partner.

Rotation has several benefits.

It:

* distributes opportunity;
* exposes students to different institutions;
* gives host institutions visibility;
* creates local ecosystems;
* avoids concentrating UY in one city;
* builds institutional relationships across Africa.

A student should eventually be able to say:

> "I've been to UY at AIMS."

or:

> "I've been to UY at UCT."

or:

> "I've been to UY at another African university."

The institution becomes part of the network.

# The Give-Back Mechanism

Physical equipment should not disappear after an activation.

When possible, participants should have several pathways.

### Path 1 — Keep building

The participant retains the equipment and continues their work.

### Path 2 — Donate

The participant gives the equipment to another student or community.

### Path 3 — Teach

The participant uses the equipment to run a workshop.

### Path 4 — Start a UY Club

The participant establishes a local activation.

### Path 5 — Mentor

The participant helps a future competition team.

### Path 6 — Return

The participant eventually accompanies a new generation to a regional, national or continental event as a mentor.

The goal is not simply:

> **Give a student a Raspberry Pi.**

The goal is:

> **Turn one Raspberry Pi into many future opportunities.**

# Entrepreneurship

UY should deliberately create a pathway from technical achievement to entrepreneurship.

A successful hackathon project should not necessarily end when the prizes are awarded.

Participants can be encouraged to ask:

> Is this useful?

> Who needs it?

> Can it become a product?

> Can it become open source?

> Can it become a company?

> Can it solve an African problem?

> Can it scale beyond Africa?

UY can provide or facilitate:

* entrepreneurship mentorship;
* business development;
* product development;
* intellectual property guidance;
* industry connections;
* founder mentorship;
* investor introductions;
* incubation partnerships.

UY does not need to become a venture capital fund.

Its role is to help **technical talent cross the bridge from idea to opportunity**.

UY should remain:

**Open.**

**Independent.**

**Technology agnostic.**

**Collaborative.**

**African in identity.**

**Global in ambition.**

**Student-driven.**

**Research-oriented.**

**Entrepreneurial.**

**Open source wherever practical.**

UY should not prescribe a single technology stack.

It should not prescribe a single methodology.

It should not require a single hardware platform.

It should not create an exclusive technology club.

The question is always:

> **What can we build, learn, research or create together?**

# International Ambition

Africa should not merely participate in international competitions.

Africa should eventually host them.

UY's long-term ambition includes developing a pathway where African students can:

1. compete locally;
2. qualify nationally;
3. compete continentally;
4. represent Africa internationally;
5. win internationally;
6. return home;
7. teach the next generation.

Eventually:

> **Africa should be a destination for global technical competitions, not merely a source of competitors.**

It represents a change in scale.

From:

**one student**

to:

**one team**

to:

**one school**

to:

**one city**

to:

**one country**

to:

**Africa**

to:

**the Global South**

**the world.**

That is an activation.

And every activation has the potential to create another.
