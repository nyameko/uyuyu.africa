# UY Africa — Legal, Governance & Registration Architecture

**Repository:** `uy.africa`
**Status:** Founding / pre-incorporation architecture
**Commit:** #4

---

# 1. Purpose

This document defines the proposed legal, financial and governance architecture for UY Africa.

UY is intended to become an independent South African non-profit organisation operating across Africa and the Global South.

The organisation will support:

* STEM;
* computing;
* scientific research;
* technology;
* youth development;
* hackathons and competitions;
* open-source development;
* research activations;
* entrepreneurship;
* education and mentorship;
* access to computing infrastructure;
* scholarships, bursaries and fellowships;
* community technology projects;
* collaboration with universities, research institutions, industry, government and civil society.

UY must remain technologically and institutionally agnostic.

The organisation's legal structure must therefore be broad enough to survive changes in technology, programmes, partners and geographical scope.

---

# 2. Legal Architecture

UY will initially pursue the following structure:

```text
                 UY AFRICA
                    │
                    ▼
          CIPC Non-Profit Company
                    │
                    ▼
       Department of Social Development
              NPO Registration
                    │
                    ▼
              SARS PBO Approval
             Section 30 / ITA
                    │
                    ▼
          SARS Section 18A Approval
                    │
                    ▼
              Institutional Bank
                    │
                    ▼
        Accounting + Governance + Audit
```

The roles of these registrations are distinct.

> **CIPC establishes the legal NPC.**

> **The MOI governs it.**

> **DSD recognises it as an NPO.**

> **SARS grants PBO tax-exempt status.**

> **SARS separately grants Section 18A approval.**

> **The bank provides the operational financial account.**

"NGO" is not another registration step. It is a general description of a non-governmental organisation. South African NPO registration can apply to trusts, companies and other associations. UY's legal vehicle will be a company.

---

# 3. Recommended Legal Vehicle

## South African Non-Profit Company

UY should be established as a:

> **Non-Profit Company without members**

with a:

> **customised Memorandum of Incorporation**

and an initial board of directors.

CIPC currently permits NPCs both with and without members and allows customised MOIs. A minimum of three incorporators/directors is required; UY should voluntarily establish a larger and more capable founding board rather than designing around the statutory minimum.

### Recommended initial board

Target:

**5–7 directors**

rather than the minimum three.

Possible competency areas:

1. Founder / technology / research
2. Education / youth development
3. Finance / accounting / audit
4. Legal / governance
5. Science / academia
6. Entrepreneurship / industry
7. African / international partnerships

The board should be selected for **competence and fiduciary responsibility**, not prestige.

---

# 4. Why NPC Rather Than Trust

UY should not initially be structured as a trust.

The NPC provides:

* a recognised corporate legal personality;
* perpetual succession;
* clear director governance;
* a natural institutional structure;
* compatibility with NPO and PBO status;
* easier institutional partnerships;
* easier grant and banking relationships;
* a structure that can scale internationally.

The trust model can be useful for particular charitable or asset-holding purposes, but it is not necessary for UY's core operating organisation.

---

# 5. Why Without Members

The recommended structure is:

> **NPC without members**

rather than creating a membership organisation.

This prevents the organisation from becoming unnecessarily political or procedural as it grows.

The board becomes responsible for stewardship while the wider UY community participates through:

* clubs;
* activations;
* volunteers;
* alumni;
* mentors;
* researchers;
* fellows;
* partners;
* contributors.

The UY community does not need to become a formal corporate membership body.

# 7. CIPC Name Reservation

Submit a name reservation application.

Current CIPC pricing:

* Name reservation: **R50**
* NPC without members, standard MOI: **R125**
* NPC without members, customised MOI: **R425**

Therefore:

> **R475 statutory CIPC starting cost**

for a reserved-name, customised NPC, before any optional professional/legal costs.

A CIPC name reservation is valid for six months.

# 9. What the MOI Should NOT Do

The MOI should not prescribe:

* Raspberry Pi;
* a particular GPU vendor;
* Kubernetes;
* Linux;
* a particular programming language;
* a particular university;
* a particular research framework;
* a particular competition;
* a particular cloud provider;
* a particular country;
* a specific hardware manufacturer.

For example:

> **One Million Raspberry Pis**

belongs in the programme portfolio.

It does not belong in the constitutional identity of UY.

The MOI should allow UY to support:

> Raspberry Pi today → RISC-V tomorrow → quantum computing later → technologies not yet invented.

# 10. Non-Profit Restrictions

The MOI should make clear that UY's income and assets exist to advance its public-benefit objectives and are not distributable to founders, directors or members except for legitimate remuneration, reimbursement and other lawful payments.

The organisation must not become a disguised private enterprise.

The Companies Act and PBO requirements govern this area.

---

# 11. Dissolution / Asset Lock

The MOI should contain a strong dissolution clause.

On winding up, remaining assets should be transferred to an appropriate qualifying non-profit/public-benefit organisation rather than distributed to founders or directors.

This is important for:

* public trust;
* NPO compliance;
* PBO approval;
* donor confidence;
* Section 18A approval.

---

# 12. Conflict of Interest

UY should establish a formal conflict-of-interest policy.

Directors and officers must disclose:

* financial interests;
* family relationships;
* related companies;
* suppliers;
* grant relationships;
* partner relationships;
* other conflicts.

A director with a material conflict should recuse themselves from relevant decisions.

# 13. DSD NPO Registration

Once the NPC exists, register it with the:

> **Department of Social Development**

as an NPO.

The South African government confirms that NPO registration is available to organisations including companies and that the service is currently free. The government indicates approximately two months as a processing timeframe, although actual processing can vary. Registered NPOs must submit annual reports within nine months of financial year-end.

The founding documents must comply with section 12 of the NPO Act or the application can be rejected.

### Public repository

The following should eventually be public:

```text
/legal/
    constitution.md
    governance.md
    public-benefit-purpose.md
    policies/
```

The actual signed statutory documents may also be published once appropriate redactions have been made.

# 14. SARS PBO

After incorporation, UY should apply to SARS for:

> **Public Benefit Organisation approval**

under section 30 of the Income Tax Act.

SARS defines a PBO as an entity whose sole or principal object is carrying on one or more qualifying public-benefit activities in a non-profit manner with an altruistic or philanthropic intent, for the benefit of or accessible to the general public or a sufficiently broad section of it.

This is an important reason to draft the MOI correctly from the beginning.

UY should be designed as a genuine public-benefit organisation, not a private technology organisation that later attempts to retrofit itself into PBO status.

---

# 15. Public-Benefit Activities

The SARS application should map UY's actual activities against the relevant categories in the Ninth Schedule.

The application should contain:

* detailed activity descriptions;
* target beneficiaries;
* geographical scope;
* implementation model;
* governance;
* funding model;
* supporting documents;
* founding document;
* financial information where required.

SARS allows an organisation established during the relevant financial year to apply without annual financial statements, subject to the applicable requirements.

---

# 16. Section 18A

UY should separately apply for:

> **Section 18A approval**

and should initially seek approval as a:

> **"doer" PBO**

rather than primarily as a conduit/funder.

A doer PBO conducts the qualifying public-benefit activities itself.

This accurately describes UY.

UY will actually:

* run hackathons;
* train and mentor;
* provide equipment;
* conduct activations;
* support research;
* provide student opportunities;
* run STEM projects.

SARS confirms that Section 18A approval can be requested simultaneously with PBO approval.

---

# 17. Section 18A Receipts

UY must NOT issue Section 18A tax-deductible receipts until SARS has actually approved Section 18A and issued the relevant reference number.

SARS explicitly states that receipts may only be issued from the date of confirmed approval.

Once approved, receipt controls must include the required information, including:

* SARS Section 18A reference;
* unique receipt number;
* donation date;
* UY details;
* donor details;
* cash amount or nature/value of an in-kind donation;
* required certification.

---

# 18. Section 18A Accounting Separation

Section 18A donations must be tracked separately.

UY should therefore maintain:

```text

Non-qualifying income
Programme-specific funds

```

This should eventually be reflected directly in the accounting chart of accounts.

# 22. Founder Funding Options

After incorporation, the board should formally decide how valid founder-paid costs are treated.

Possible treatments:

### A. Reimbursement

UY reimburses the founder.

### B. Founder loan

The founder records the amount as a loan/advance to UY, repayable subject to the organisation's finances and board approval.

### C. Donation

The founder voluntarily contributes the money to UY and does not seek repayment.

For the initial formation phase, **reimbursement or a clearly documented founder advance is preferable for accounting clarity** unless the founder deliberately intends to donate the money.

The board should approve the treatment.

---

# 23. When Does UY "Own" the Expense?

Before incorporation:

> The founder personally incurs the expense.

After incorporation:

> UY becomes a separate legal person.

Therefore, as soon as the NPC exists, expenses should be incurred directly by UY wherever possible.

For pre-incorporation expenses that genuinely relate to forming UY, maintain a documented schedule and have the new board review and approve the appropriate accounting treatment.

Do not simply move money backwards and forwards without documentation.

---

# 24. When Does UY Officially Start?

There are several different milestones.

### Legal existence

When CIPC incorporates the NPC.

### NPO status

When DSD issues the NPO certificate.

### PBO status

When SARS approves section 30 PBO status.

### Section 18A capability

When SARS confirms Section 18A approval.

### First project

UY may begin conducting legitimate public-benefit activities once the legal organisation exists and governance/financial controls are in place.

There is no reason to wait until UY has achieved Section 18A approval before doing anything.

---

# 25. Fundraising Before Section 18A

UY may seek funding and donations subject to its legal and tax position.

However:

> **Do not tell donors that their donation is Section 18A tax deductible until UY actually has Section 18A approval.**

Until then, describe funding accurately as:

> donation / sponsorship / grant / contribution

and not:

> Section 18A tax-deductible donation.

Once approved, use the official Section 18A process.

# 34. Accounting

## Initial recommendation: GnuCash

UY should initially use:

> **GnuCash**

GnuCash is free and open-source, provides double-entry accounting, runs locally and is available for Linux and other major operating systems. Its source is publicly available on GitHub.

This is ideal for the first stage because:

* no subscription;
* no vendor lock-in;
* local data;
* open source;
* double-entry accounting;
* exportable data;
* easy backups;
* suitable for a small organisation.

### Do not build an accounting system yet.

This is one area where UY should resist the temptation to turn itself into a software project.

# 38. Intellectual Property

CIPC company-name registration is **not trademark registration**.

A company name and a trademark are legally distinct. CIPC explicitly states that registering a company does not automatically register its trademark.

Therefore UY should separately investigate trademark protection for:

* **UY**
* **UY.AFRICA**
* **UYUYU**
* the UYUYU logo/symbol
* potentially important slogans

The exact classes should be selected based on actual intended use.

CIPC allows trademark registration across the Nice classification system, and a registered trademark can be renewed indefinitely in ten-year periods.

> **Core UY intellectual property belongs to UY where appropriate.**

> "It's on GitHub, so it's open source."

# 40. Domain Ownership

The `uy.africa` domain should eventually be controlled by the organisation rather than remaining permanently dependent on the founder's personal account.

During the founding period, the founder may acquire and hold it temporarily.

Once UY has a legal entity and bank account:

1. document ownership;
2. transfer control to the organisation;
3. ensure multiple trusted administrators;
4. enable MFA;
5. maintain recovery procedures.

The .africa registry confirms that pricing is set by registrars and that premium short names may have different pricing.

Current 2026 comparisons show standard .africa registration offers ranging roughly from US$6–23 depending on registrar, with renewal prices varying considerably.

The exact price for `uy.africa` should therefore be checked directly with an accredited registrar before assuming it is standard-priced.

# 46. Public/Private Boundary

The repository itself should be governed by one principle:

> ## Open by default. Private by necessity.

The public repository contains the **methodology, philosophy, governance architecture, templates, code and lessons**.

The private organisational systems contain **people, money, contracts, security and confidential information**.

UY should demonstrate that an organisation can be built transparently without compromising the privacy and security of the people who participate in it.

---

# 47. Founding Philosophy

UY is not merely registering an organisation.

It is demonstrating a model.

> **An African organisation can be built openly.**

> **Young people can participate in building the organisation itself.**

> **Technology can be developed with modest resources.**

> **Knowledge can be shared rather than hoarded.**

> **Research, education, entrepreneurship and community development can reinforce one another.**

> **The organisation's institutional memory can itself be open source.**

And ultimately:

> **The organisation should become more capable than its founder.**

That is the measure of successful institutional design.
